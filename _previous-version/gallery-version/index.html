About Me

Hello! My name is Your Name, and I am a course and year student. I grew up hearing stories at home: folk tales from my grandparents, teleserye plots retold at the dinner table, and the legends we learned in grade school. Stories have always been the way my family made sense of the world. This course gave me the chance to look at much older stories and ask the same questions people asked thousands of years ago.

Before this class, I thought of epics as long, difficult poems that only scholars read. I was wrong. Once I looked past the unfamiliar names, I found families arguing, friends grieving, leaders making mistakes, and ordinary people trying to do the right thing. These works feel ancient, but the people in them feel surprisingly close.

What I Bring to Reading

I read best when I connect a story to real life. I like to ask what a character wants, what stands in their way, and what the story is quietly teaching. I also pay attention to culture and art: how a story reflects the beliefs of the people who first told it, and how painters across the centuries have imagined its scenes.

Interests: mythology, Philippine folklore, painting and design, music, history, and films based on books.

My Goal for This Portfolio

This portfolio is a record of how my understanding grew. I want to show not only what each work is about, but what each one taught me. I hope a reader finishes it believing, as I now do, that these old stories still speak to a student in the Philippines today.

The Seven Works Through Time

These works were composed across almost two thousand years. Arranged from oldest to youngest, they show how storytellers in different lands kept returning to the same questions.

    The Iliad
