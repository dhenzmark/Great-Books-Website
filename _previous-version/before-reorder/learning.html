My reflection

SUMMARY F LEARNING

These seven works came from different places and centuries, yet they kept answering the same human questions. Here are the most important ideas I learned and how the works connect.

Pictures above: bust of Homer, The British Museum, and Achille-Etna Michallon, Landscape with the Death of Roland (sketch), c. 1819–22.

ΣΟΦΙΑ
01

Literature mirrors culture

Each work shows what its people valued most. Greece prized honor and cleverness, India prized dharma, medieval France prized feudal loyalty, and the Hebrew tradition wrestled with faith and justice.

02

Heroism has many faces

Achilles is a hero of strength, Odysseus of wit, Rama of duty, Roland of loyalty, and Job of endurance. The Panchatantra even makes heroes of a mouse and a tortoise.

03

Duty and desire collide

Rama gives up a throne for his father’s word, and Arjuna must fight his own family. The hardest choices are between two things we care about.

04

Pride brings downfall

Achilles’ rage, Roland’s refusal to blow the horn, Duryodhana’s envy, Ravana’s desire, and Odysseus’ boast all lead to suffering.

05

Suffering can bring wisdom

Job, Odysseus, the Pandavas, and Priam all pass through great loss. Faced honestly, suffering can deepen understanding and compassion.

06

Stories travel

The Panchatantra crossed into Persia and Europe, the Ramayana became Maharadia Lawana in Mindanao, and Charlemagne’s knights inspired our moro-moro plays.

What I learned about literary craft

Before this course, I read mainly for plot. Now I notice how a story is told. Many of these works began as oral literature, performed from memory long before they were written down. That explains Homer’s repeated epithets and the repeated laisses of The Song of Roland, which helped performers remember and listeners follow.

I learned to recognize techniques across cultures. The frame narrative appears in Job, the Panchatantra, and the Mahabharata. Beginning in medias res drives both Homeric epics. Divine intervention shapes nearly every work, from Athena guiding Odysseus to Krishna guiding Arjuna.

I also learned to read critically. Sita’s trial by fire and the portrayal of Muslims in Roland reflect attitudes we should question today. Appreciating a masterpiece does not mean accepting everything in it.
