Welcome

Student profile

Hello! My name is Dhenz Mark A. Alden, and I am a 4th Year BS Information Technology student. I grew up hearing stories at home: folk tales from my grandparents, teleserye plots retold at the dinner table, and the legends we learned in grade school. Stories have always been the way my family made sense of the world. This course gave me the chance to look at much older stories and ask the same questions people asked thousands of years ago.

Before this class, I thought of epics as long, difficult poems that only scholars read. I was wrong. Once I looked past the unfamiliar names, I found families arguing, friends grieving, leaders making mistakes, and ordinary people trying to do the right thing.

Read my reflection
DA

I read best when I connect a story to real life. I ask what a character wants, what stands in their way, and what the story is quietly teaching.

I also pay attention to culture and art: how a story reflects the beliefs of the people who first told it, and how painters across the centuries have imagined its scenes.

Interests: mythology, Philippine folklore, painting and design, music, history, and films based on books.

The seven works

Each one hangs here like an exhibition. Open any of them to read the full analysis.

Voice out of the Whirlwind

Hebrew wisdom literature, c. 6th–4th century BCE

A righteous man loses everything and receives a storm of questions instead of answers.

The Panchatantra

Ancient India, c. 200 BCE – 300 CE

Animal fables that turn three dull princes into wise rulers in six months.

The Ramayana

Ancient India, c. 5th century BCE – 1st century CE

A prince keeps his father’s word, then crosses an ocean to rescue his wife.

The Mahabharata

Ancient India, c. 400 BCE – 400 CE

Cousins go to war, and a warrior asks his god what duty really means.

The Iliad

Ancient Greece, c. 8th century BCE

The greatest warrior of the Greeks learns too late what his anger costs.

The Odyssey

Ancient Greece, late 8th century BCE

A clever king fights gods and monsters for ten years to get home.

The Song of Roland

Medieval France, c. 1100 CE

A proud knight refuses to call for help until it is too late.
